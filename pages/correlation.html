Correlation

The estimator, the power calculation and the multiple-comparison correction — so that when a correlation is published it is defensible. No value is published today, because the joint-outcome dataset does not exist.

No correlation value is published.

A number invented here would be indistinguishable from a guess. Everything below is the procedure that must be followed before any value appears — including the estimator, the interval and the test.

The estimator

p_A = E[1{A}]  p_B = E[1{B}]  p_AB = E[1{A}·1{B}] Cov(A,B) = p_AB − p_A·p_B ρ = Cov(A,B) / (σ_A·σ_B)  defined only when σ_A, σ_B > 0

If a leg has zero variance (always wins or always loses), ρ is undefined — not zero. Report it as undefined and drop it from the pricing model.

Why the sign is not enough

Correlation gives the direction; the parlay's fair price needs the joint probability p_AB, and fair odds = 1/p_AB. Two legs can share a ρ and still need different prices at different base rates. Publish p_AB with an interval next to ρ, or publish nothing.

The independence trap, in numbers

Two legs at p = 0.5 each, both priced at −110. For binary legs the joint is exact once ρ is chosen: p_AB = 0.25 + 0.25ρ at p = 0.5.

Estimation procedure (mandatory)

  1. Define the setting. A correlation measured across different conditions is an average of things that do not pool.
  2. Train on the past only. Chronological split; a random split leaks the future into the past.
  3. Bootstrap the interval. Report 95% intervals on ρ and p_AB. With ~17 games per club per season, intervals are wide.
  4. Test the independence null with a likelihood-ratio or permutation test; publish the p-value and n.
  5. Confirm out of sample. A correlation that exists only in the fitting window is noise with a bibliography.
  6. Correct for multiple comparisons. Test 200 leg pairs and ~10 clear p < 0.05 by chance; use a Benjamini–Hochberg style correction.

Sample-size reality check

n ≈ ((z_(1−α/2) + z_(1−β)) / z_ρ)² + 3  →  ≈780 paired observations for ρ = 0.10 at 80% power

A season is 272 games, and conditioning on game state shrinks the usable n further. Any correlation published from a single season of a single team's props should be treated as a hypothesis, not a finding.
