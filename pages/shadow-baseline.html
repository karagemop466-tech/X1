Read the lower bound, not the point estimate. Skill of +0.116 with a 95% interval of [+0.002, +0.230] on 117 games means the baseline is probably better than a no-information forecast and only just provably so. A single season of 117 games cannot support more than that sentence, and anything stronger on this page would be a fabrication. Multiplicity is disclosed: n_trials = 1, so the Bonferroni factor is 0.05 — which is honest only while nobody scores a second variant on this window without registering it first.

The removal-of-luck panel

A bettor who always takes the model’s side went 75–42 where its own probabilities expected 76.057 wins. The standard deviation of that difference is 5.042 wins, so the gap is -0.21 standard deviations — inside one standard deviation: this record is not evidence of skill or of failure, it is what a coin would do. Win totals are luck until they are compared with the sum of the probabilities behind them; that comparison is this panel, and it is the reason the leaderboard separates signal from staking.
