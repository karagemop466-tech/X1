Availability & depth fragility

The official injury report tells you who is listed. It does not tell you who is a starter, who is second string, or who replaces whom. This page separates those two facts — and shows exactly where the second one comes from.

The finding that matters most.

The official NFL injury report prints five things per player: player, position, injury, practice status and game status. It prints no depth-chart rank at all. “2nd-string replacement” therefore cannot be read off the official record. Clubs publish their own charts, and at least Detroit and Green Bay label theirs “unofficial”.

Full-coverage join: every injured player whose club publishes a chart (258 rows)

This is the join the pilot above is a subset of. It uses the club depth charts already collected for 31 of 32 clubs — the missing one is Houston, whose chart returns a page with no parsable table (attempt log in data/pipeline/_run_report.json). Depth 1 means the player is the first name printed at his position on his own club's chart; it is a club self-report, not a league fact, and every row carries the club URL so you can check it.

What the extra coverage changes.

The pilot reports 7 starters ruled Out. The same rule across the 31 clubs already collected returns 30, plus 16 second-to-fourth-string players listed Out or Doubtful. 89 injured players could not be matched: 89 are from Houston (no chart) or are not printed on their club's chart — those rows are absent here rather than guessed.

Q-19 asks whether this join should replace the pilot on the site permanently. This release publishes it and keeps both, so the decision can be made on the evidence rather than on a description.
