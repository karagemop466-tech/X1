Signals, correlations & validation

The goal is disciplined discovery, not a narrative dressed up as an edge. Every strategy below is a hypothesis to test. No strategy has been run here; estimates, thresholds, correlations, and returns remain blank.

No luck-removal claim: a model cannot remove random variation from a game. It can limit overconfidence by predicting probabilities, modeling dependence, reporting uncertainty, charging all costs, comparing against honest baselines, and requiring prospective replication.

For Pearson correlation, NIST defines the usual coefficient on a −1 to +1 scale and describes it as measuring strength of (usually linear) association. Correlation is not causation; for binary/thresholded market legs, select a suitable dependence measure rather than mechanically reporting Pearson r.

Leaderboard concept

Use separate transparent leaderboards for (1) probability forecasting, (2) single-market paper performance, and (3) parlay/joint-probability forecasting; do not collapse these into a single score before the user chooses the objective.

  • Show proper probability scoring and calibration alongside market-return metrics; they answer different questions.
  • Report ROI only with the precise price timestamp, settlement, sample size, and uncertainty; do not crown a winner from raw win percentage.
  • Use a predeclared minimum activity/time requirement and a locked evaluation window; the threshold itself is not set.
  • Display all strategy versions, suppressed/voided picks, and changes. Use uncertainty-aware rank bands rather than implying a tiny rank gap is meaningful.
  • Rewards remain disabled pending written terms and legal review. This site does not endorse or guarantee wagering outcomes.
