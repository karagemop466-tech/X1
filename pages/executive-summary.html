Executive summary

What is verified, what is deliberately absent, and the decisions that gate everything else.

Snapshot: 2026-11-14 · 2026 NFL season, Week 10. This document describes the state of the repository at that date; every quantitative table in it is a single-week snapshot and will go stale.

The one-paragraph version

This release does the two things a betting-strategy project usually skips: it establishes a verified factual base, and it refuses to publish a number it cannot source. Thirty-two teams' Week 10 standings, all 14 Week 10 games, 345 official injury-report rows and five clubs' depth charts are transcribed with per-row links to the official page and mechanically re-checked by 2,944 integrity assertions. Against that base the project publishes a complete specification for originating lines, removing luck, pricing correlation and running a skill-based leaderboard — and publishes zero prices, zero correlation values and zero strategy results, because the licensed data those require does not exist here. The gap is enumerated as 24 numbered decisions.

The five findings that actually matter

  1. There is no league-wide official depth chart.

    The NFL publishes none. Clubs publish their own, and Detroit and Green Bay print the word “unofficial” on the page. Any claim about 2nd-, 3rd- or 4th-string replacements must be labelled a club self-report, not a league fact. This is the biggest constraint on the availability requirement.

  2. “Starter” is a definition you choose, not a fact you look up.

    This release defines it mechanically — printed in column 1 of the club's chart — so the definition is auditable and re-runnable rather than an editorial judgement per player.

  3. A 55% hit rate over a season is not skill.

    At −110 the break-even is 52.38%. 55.1% over 500 bets scores z ≈ 1.22, which is inside coin-flip range. The site shows this arithmetic so a hot record cannot be mistaken for an edge.

  4. Parlay margin compounds.

    At −110 one leg carries a 4.55% hold; five legs carry 20.75% — about 4.6×. And assuming independence across correlated legs misprices the ticket: two 50% legs with ρ = 0.30 have a joint probability of 0.325, so a book paying +264 is handing the bettor roughly 18.5%. That is precisely why books price or cap correlated legs. Prop combos are priced from a joint distribution or not priced at all.

  5. Verification caught a real error of ours.

    Arizona was first transcribed as 2–6; the official page prints 3–6 with .333. An arithmetic gate rejected the row, the correction is corroborated by two independent listings, and it is documented publicly as flag F-08.

Honest limitations of this release

  • The injury report and schedule are a single-week snapshot and will be stale within days.
  • Depth coverage is 5 of 32 clubs; the other 27 are blank on purpose.
  • One transcription error was found by our own gate. A second independent pass (suggestion S-10) is the cheapest remaining trust improvement and has not been done.
  • Three other agent sessions touched this repository concurrently; their divergent boards are preserved on their branches and audited as F-07.
  • No model has been fitted, so no claimed signal has empirical support. They are hypotheses with named tests, which is what a hypothesis is supposed to be.
