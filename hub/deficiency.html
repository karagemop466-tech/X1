How a row is built. The starter is column 1 of the club's own depth chart. The replacement is the next name on that chart who is not himself ruled Out on the official injury report, and the "string" is that name's position on the chart — 2nd, 3rd or 4th. Nothing here is guessed: the chart is the club's, the designation is the league's, and both are linked from every row. Where the club prints no further name at that position, the cell says so instead of inventing a backup.
Club charts are self-reported. Several clubs label their own depth chart "unofficial" and update it on their own schedule. packers.com, for example, ends with "Unofficial, Nov. 10, 2026". Treat the replacement column as the club's current stated intention, not as a league-verified fact.

Reading it for a decision

A starter ruled Out with a 2nd-string replacement is the ordinary case and carries little signal — the market and every other model can see it too. The rows worth attention are the ones where the replacement is a 3rd or 4th string, where the replacement is himself Questionable, or where the club prints no replacement at all. Those are the positions where the public information is thinnest, and they are the only places where a depth-based signal could plausibly be worth anything.
