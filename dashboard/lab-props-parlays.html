Props & Parlays — Policy, Correlation Rules, and What Is Blocked

1. Current status of player props: BLOCKED (honest)

Generating fair prop lines (passing yards, rushing, receiving, TD scorer, etc.) requires player usage data: targets, carries, routes, air yards, team tendencies. That data lives in play-by-play / official stat feeds which are not available in this environment (nflverse release assets blocked from the sandbox — see methodology, OQ-4).

Therefore:

  • No prop lines are published. Fields that would need them are blank and flagged.
  • Player-level correlation values are not published — they cannot be measured without play-level outcomes. Guessing correlation coefficients would violate the no-inference rule.
  • Discovery options are logged in open-questions.html Q4.

2. Parlay policy (game-level — supported by measured data)

Measured facts (computed, 1999–2026): weekly home-cover variance ratio 0.99 → outcomes across games behave like independent binomials; P(home cover | over) − P(home cover) = +0.3pp → spread and total legs are near-independent at game level.

Rules: 1. Cross-game parlays only. Pricing multiplies leg probabilities (justified by the measured independence). 2. Correlation cap 0.35 (proposed): any two legs with measured correlation above the cap are rejected. 3. No same-game parlays until player-level correlations are measured from play-by-play data (classic priors — QB passing ↔ WR receiving positive, RB rushing ↔ WR receiving negative, team total ↔ player props positive — are unverified hypotheses, not inputs). 4. Parlay EV must clear the same threshold as singles; vig compounding is not an edge. 5. "Player prop bet combos" remain blocked until (1) prop fair lines exist and (2) same-game correlations are measured.
