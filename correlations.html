Correlation: Methodology & Values

How bet-leg correlation is measured, why it changes parlay and prop-combo math, and the interpretation framework. Actual correlation coefficients are computed from historical data and are not yet available — they are shown blank and flagged rather than guessed.

Why correlation matters

A parlay or prop combo assumes each leg is independent, then multiplies probabilities. But legs drawn from the same game (or same passing attack) are correlated — when a QB has a big passing day, his WRs and the over both tend to hit together. Ignoring that correlation overstates or understates the true probability of the combo, which corrupts the edge and the G5 correlation gate. Correlation is therefore a required input to honest multi-leg pricing.

How correlation is measured

  1. Define leg outcomes — settle each leg (win/loss, or the stat vs. its line) over a historical window.
  2. Pair them — for every combo type (e.g., QB passing yards + WR receiving yards), collect matched historical outcomes.
  3. Compute the coefficient — Pearson (linear, continuous stats) or Spearman (rank-based, robust to outliers) correlation between the legs.
  4. Roll forward — recompute over rolling windows so correlation is refreshed and point-in-time, not stale.
  5. Apply it — use the measured coefficient to adjust the combo's true probability instead of the independence product.

This is methodology only. The coefficients below require historical data that has not yet been compiled for this site (see Q3).

Interpretation framework (conventional guide)

Conventional interpretation of a correlation coefficient r. This is a general statistical guide, not an NFL-specific measured value.

Range of |r|InterpretationImplication for a multi-leg bet
0.00 – 0.10NegligibleIndependence assumption is reasonable.
0.10 – 0.29WeakSmall effect; independence is a mild approximation.
0.30 – 0.49ModerateIndependence materially misprices the combo; apply correlation.
0.50 – 0.74StrongLegs move together; correlation must be modeled explicitly.
0.75 – 1.00Very strongLegs are nearly redundant; combo offers little diversification.
