Capture fields before drawing conclusions

  • Event/team, date, official position, player identity, source URL, retrieved timestamp (UTC), report effective time, and source version.
  • Practice participation and game designation recorded separately; preserve successive updates. Mark unavailable only when the relevant official source states it, and retain the original text.
  • Depth chart/starting assignment source and timestamp; list known replacements by observed role/snap share only when sourced. Do not label a second-, third-, or fourth-string player without an explicit authoritative source.
  • Offensive and defensive unit impact: role and expected workload, unit dependencies (e.g. line continuity), and uncertainty. Keep observed facts distinct from model estimates.
  • Source disagreement, stale page, missing report, inactive list not yet posted, or ambiguous identity becomes a visible review flag—not a guessed status.

Why depth matters, and what is not established

NFL Football Operations describes an on-field injury spotting process as an alert to club medical staff, not a diagnosis. This underscores that injury observations, official game status, and a model's estimate of availability are separate concepts. The system should not infer medical severity or return probability. Estimating football impact needs role, participation, player-level historical performance, replacement usage, opponent and scheme context, and uncertainty. None of those impact values are available in this repository.
