Originating our own lines

The brief is to originate our own NFL lines rather than reuse someone else's numbers. This page explains what that requires, which inputs are verified today, and why the market comparison column is empty.

Market lines table is intentionally empty. No official NFL or club source publishes spreads, totals, moneylines or player-prop prices. Under the verification rule a value that cannot be confirmed from an official source stays blank and is flagged — so this table ships with no rows rather than with numbers recalled from somewhere. Source of that finding: the probe report on the questions page, and nfl.com ↗, which publishes no betting prices.

Market lines

What we can originate today

Originating a line means producing a fair number from verified inputs. Three of the four inputs are already official and machine-readable:

Verified and available
  • Team scoring and efficiency — official scoring stats ↗
  • Points for and against — official standings ↗
  • Injury status and the depth of the replacement — official injury report ↗ plus each club's own depth chart
  • Home and road splits — official standings
Missing, and blocking the comparison
  • Market prices: no official source. Blocks the edge column and closing line value (Q-02).
  • Game results: no official machine-readable source. Blocks calibration and backtesting (Q-04).
  • Play-by-play: no official source verified. Blocks pace and success-rate inputs (Q-21).

The intended pipeline

  1. Estimate team strength from official scoring, net points and home/road splits. All inputs verified.
  2. Adjust for lineup using the official injury report and the club's own depth chart, weighted by how far down the chart the replacement comes from — a 4th-string replacement is not the same event as a 2nd-string one.
  3. Convert to a fair number — a spread and a total for each game.
  4. Compare with the market and record the difference as the edge. Blocked on Q-02.
  5. Record the closing number so the signal can be scored on closing line value without waiting for the result. Blocked on Q-02.
  6. Version every line with the timestamp of the inputs that produced it, so any later claim about the edge can be checked. Proposed as S-19.

Parlays and player-prop combinations

Correlation is what separates a rational parlay from a worse version of a single bet. Two rules follow from the numbers already computed:

  • Every leg must clear the single-bet edge gate on its own (gate G-08). Parlaying a negative-edge leg makes the bet worse, not better, whatever the correlation is.
  • Legs may be combined only when the correlation between them is measured and either below the threshold or explicitly priced in (gate G-07). At team level that can be done today — see the correlation values. Within a single game, or between two player props, it cannot: no official source publishes the data (Q-10, Q-11).
Player-prop combination strategy ST-07 is marked Blocked, not because the idea is bad but because no official source for prop-level data was verified. It stays blocked rather than being approximated.
