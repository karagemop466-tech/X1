Removing luck from the equation

Three separate mechanisms here: strip wins that arrive in specific closing-game states, require a sample large enough for the effect to be visible, and correct for the fact that many rules were tested at once. Without the third, the first two are theatre.

1. The multiplicity correction

16 strategies were scored. At α = 0.05 a family of 16 independent tests produces on average 0.80 false positives. The Bonferroni threshold is α/k = 0.003125.

2. The sample size a real edge needs

Break-even at -110 is 52.38%. To detect a 55% capper at 95% confidence with 80% power:

n ≈ 2,837 settled bets. At 500 bets the same 55% record is z = 1.17 — indistinguishable from chance. This is not a conservative assumption; it is the arithmetic that makes a hot month meaningless.

3. The luck-removed panel

Wins that arrive in the closing-game states listed in the recorded rule are excluded and the ROI recomputed. The luck profit share is the fraction of total profit that came from those wins. A strategy whose profit share is high is a strategy whose profit is partly an artefact of how the game ended, not of the number.

Note the direction of the evidence in the table above: the one strategy that clears p ≤ 0.05 has a higher ROI after luck removal, which is weak evidence that its edge is not a lucky-win artefact — and still not enough to survive the multiplicity correction.
