Open Questions & Information Gaps

The questions that must be answered — and the information we have vs. lack — before any decision can reasonably be made. Each gap includes a suggested path to discover the missing information. These gate the recommendations on the Suggestions board.

Have — verified Lack — to discover Decision blocked until answered
Q1. Which data feeds do we standardize on for play-by-play, EPA, and betting lines?
What we haveVerified standings, Week-10 injury report, and Week-10 market lines from official/authoritative sources (see Sources).
What we lackA selected, end-to-end historical feed covering multiple seasons of play-by-play, EPA, depth charts, and closing lines in one normalized schema.
Why it mattersEvery model, correlation estimate, and validation test depends on consistent historical data. Choosing the wrong feed risks look-ahead bias and irreproducible results.
How to discoverCompare candidate official/authoritative feeds for coverage, granularity, and update frequency; document the selection and its limitations on the Sources page.
Q2. What is the minimum sample size before a strategy is considered for the leaderboard?
What we haveA defined validation-gate framework (see Validation Gates) — but no numeric thresholds set yet.
What we lackA chosen minimum number of bets/seasons required to distinguish skill from variance.
Why it mattersToo small a sample lets luck through; too large a sample delays useful feedback. This directly controls the "remove luck" requirement.
How to discoverSet the threshold from the required confidence interval width and expected edge size; run a sensitivity analysis on historical data once a feed is chosen.
Q3. How do we measure and apply bet-leg correlation for parlays and prop combos?
What we haveA correlation methodology and interpretation framework (see Correlations).
What we lackActual pairwise correlation coefficients computed from historical data.
Why it mattersParlay and prop-combo true probability depends on leg correlation; assuming independence when legs are correlated misprices risk and inflates apparent edge.
How to discoverCompute pairwise correlations over rolling historical windows once historical play/prop data is available; store and refresh them as snapshots.
Q4. What is the primary leaderboard ranking metric, and how are rewards tiered?
What we haveA leaderboard design with validation-gated qualification (see Leaderboard).
What we lackA final decision on the ranking metric (ROI vs. CLV vs. units vs. a blend) and the reward tiers.
Why it mattersThe metric defines what "top performing" means and must be luck-adjusted and comparable across single bets, parlays, and props.
How to discoverEvaluate candidate metrics on historical data for robustness; pick one that is invariant to bet type and resistant to survivorship bias.
Q5. How do we quantify the impact of a starter being replaced by a 2nd/3rd/4th-string backup?
What we haveVerified player-level injury data with game status (see Injuries & Depth) and official depth-chart order.
What we lackA calibrated numeric impact (in points/EPA) per substitution, by position and string depth.
Why it mattersThis is a core edge signal; without a quantified impact we cannot convert injury/depth data into a line adjustment.
How to discoverRegress expected point outcomes on depth-chart substitution events over history; separate by position group and string depth to estimate per-event impact.
Q6. What model approach do we use to originate our own lines?
What we haveRequirement to originate independent lines (see Requirements D-01) and the market lines as a benchmark.
What we lackA selected model family (e.g., EPA-based point-differential model) and its feature set.
Why it mattersThe line-generation model is the foundation of every strategy; its design determines signal quality and CLV.
How to discoverBenchmark candidate models out-of-sample against the closing market line (CLV) before committing; document feature provenance to avoid leakage.
Q7. How do we guard against look-ahead and data leakage in validation?
What we haveWalk-forward / out-of-sample gate requirement (see Validation Gates).
What we lackA concrete leakage audit checklist (e.g., confirming injury/line info is used only as available at bet time).
Why it mattersLook-ahead bias produces artificially strong results that fail in live use; it is the main way "luck" masquerades as skill.
How to discoverBuild a time-aligned data model and an explicit audit of each feature's availability date relative to bet placement.
Q8. Where do player-prop lines and historical prop results come from?
What we haveRequirement to support prop-combo strategies (see Requirements E-03).
What we lackA captured, verified prop-line feed and historical prop settlement data.
Why it mattersPlayer-prop strategies cannot be validated or scored without prop lines and settled results.
How to discoverSelect an authoritative prop feed, capture current lines with source links, and backfill settled historical props for validation.
Q9. How do we handle line movement between open and close?
What we haveWeek-10 opening and current lines from market sources (see Market Lines).
What we lackA defined rule for which line (open or close) is the benchmark for CLV and how movement is interpreted as information.
Why it mattersCLV is measured against the closing line; movement itself can be a signal, but mis-specifying the benchmark corrupts the skill measure.
How to discoverStandardize on the closing line for CLV and separately model open→close movement as an information signal, validated out-of-sample.
Q10. What are the compliance and risk constraints on the system?
What we haveRisk-control gate requirement (max sizing, drawdown limits, no martingale) in the validation framework.
What we lackExplicit numeric risk limits and confirmation of the applicable jurisdiction/compliance constraints.
Why it mattersStrategy sizing and reward design must respect risk limits and any applicable rules; this is a prerequisite to any live use.
How to discoverSet explicit risk limits as validation-gate parameters and confirm jurisdictional requirements before enabling live scoring.
Decision rule. Until Q1–Q3 are answered, the correlation-dependent strategies (parlays, prop combos) cannot be reliably built, and until Q2 and Q7 are answered, no strategy can be admitted to the leaderboard. These are the blocking gaps; the rest are sequencing decisions.
