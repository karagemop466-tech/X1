Validation gates

Eight gates, pre-registered, and the demotion gate that returns a live strategy to paper. Their whole job is to stop luck being paid as skill.

Why the gates are built this way

  • Sample sufficiency (G1, ≥ 100 bets). Below that, a single lucky weekend moves ROI by more than any plausible edge.
  • Out-of-sample integrity (G2). Parameters are chosen from earlier seasons only; a bet may not appear twice.
  • Significance (G3). Bootstrap 95% interval lower bound above zero and a sign-flip randomisation p ≤ 0.05. The randomisation test replaced an earlier permutation test that was mathematically inert — permuting a sample cannot change its mean.
  • Economics (G4, ROI ≥ 2%). Significant but tiny is not investable after costs.
  • Baselines (G5). Must beat every always-on baseline on the same window.
  • Luck robustness (G6). Must stay positive once one-score games (margin ≤ 8) and blowouts (margin ≥ 25) are removed. Those two buckets are where variance, not skill, decides.
  • Parameter stability (G7). At least 60% of neighbouring parameter values must also be positive, so the result is not an artefact of one chosen cut-off.
  • Non-redundancy (G8, |r| ≤ 0.85). A strategy that is the same bet as an accepted one adds no information and earns no reward.

D1–D5 — the same demotion gate with concrete thresholds (second design)

Two parallel sessions defined the demotion gate on the same day. G8 above is one design (six mechanical triggers). This is the other: five triggers with explicit numeric thresholds — CLV decay, drawdown, hit-rate decay, a data-integrity trip, and the re-promotion rule (50 new graded bets after the demotion date, pre-demotion data not reusable). The two agree on the principle — any one trigger demotes automatically; re-entry is earned, never automatic — and differ in exact thresholds. The owner's choice between the threshold sets is recorded as an open decision on the decision register; nothing is lost either way. Both read the signal block of the leaderboard, never the staking block.

What a G-D1 demotion does — and how a strategy returns

  • no new live stakes on the strategy
  • open positions settle normally from the verified results feed
  • the strategy returns to the paper pool and keeps its full history
  • the leaderboard records the trigger id, the window and the date — the entry is never deleted

Re-promotion.

  • a fresh pre-registered window is required (hypothesis, inputs, market and staking rule recorded before grading, gate G2)
  • the old sample cannot be reused to pass gates
  • all eight promotion gates (G1-G8) must pass on the new window
  • the owner ratifies the return to live (decision Q-25)

the demotion gate reads SIGNAL metrics only (flat-stake ROI, CLV, gate failures); a lucky or unlucky staking plan can neither trigger nor prevent a demotion (see data/leaderboard_split.json) an override is an owner decision recorded on the suggestion board before it happens (decision Q-25); no silent overrides
