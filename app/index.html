What this system does

X1 collects NFL data from official sources only, tracks injuries and the exact string of backup who has to replace an injured starter, originates its own lines, and scores candidate strategies on a leaderboard behind eight validation gates. It runs unattended: a scheduled workflow re-pulls every official source, re-verifies every row, and republishes this site.

Verified today

  • Standings — all 32 clubs, each row checked against the league's own arithmetic.
  • Week 10 slate — 14 games, 4 byes, one final score.
  • Injuries — the league's official report, 345 rows.
  • Depth charts — 1st through 4th string from each club's own site.
  • Deficiency watch — which starter is down and which backup takes the snaps.
  • Team stats — nine official offence and defence tables.
  • Results & ROI — the verified results feed (137 settled games, double-entry checked against the standings) and the ROI it makes computable.
  • Origination & prices — side, then total, then moneyline; fair line, market line and difference as separate columns; the opening/closing price-snapshot store.
  • Results feed (all seasons) — 2019–2026, one tier label per row, cross-checks C1–C4.
  • Price history — opening and closing for every market we originate across the full backtest window, blanks flagged.

Deliberately blank

  • Market lines — no official source publishes prices, so the table ships empty; the price-snapshot store records the captures we do have with their provenance and keeps opening slots reserved and blank.
  • Player-prop prices — no official prop-level source exists.
  • Weather, officials, travel — no citable official feed found, so no values are shown.

These are not oversights. A value that cannot be confirmed from an official source stays blank and is flagged, never inferred.

The five findings that shape the plan

  1. No league-wide official depth chart exists. Clubs publish their own, in their own format, with their own "as of" date. Chicago's chart reads "As of 10/31/2026" — two weeks stale — and Green Bay's is labelled "Unofficial, Nov. 10, 2026". So "2nd-string replacement" is a club self-report, not a league fact. See flagged.
  2. The name join between the chart and the injury report is fragile, and it was silently failing for Green Bay. Green Bay prints jersey numbers in the name column ("75 Sean Rhyan"), and other clubs print or omit generational suffixes ("Kenneth Walker" vs "Kenneth Walker III"). Correcting the join moved matched starters from 171 to 189 and recovered three Green Bay starters the league lists as Out. See deficiency watch.
  3. Most of our own model is not yet good enough to beat the market. The walk-forward points model has a spread MAE of 10.42 against the market benchmark's 9.73, and threshold sweeps return ROI between −6.1% and −2.6%. It is published as a screen, not a price. See originated lines.
  4. One strategy out of sixteen passes every gate. "Wind — under" returns +11.5% ROI over 398 out-of-sample bets with a bootstrap interval that stays above zero and p = 0.008. Everything else fails significance, economics, or the luck-removal gate. See leaderboard.
  5. One input still blocks the rest: prices. The verified results feed closed the outcomes gap (137 settled games, double-entry checked — see Results & ROI), but no official or licensed source publishes betting prices, so CLV, recorded-price ROI and the price-dependent gates stay unenforced rather than filled with a guess. See decisions Q-02.

How the verification rule is enforced

On every row

  • Each row carries the URL of the official page the values were read from.
  • A value the source does not publish renders as an em dash.
  • A row with a missing value or an integrity flag is marked and listed on Flagged for review.

On every refresh

  • tools/verify_official.py re-checks every published value against the official page fetched on 2026-11-14 and writes a row-level ledger.
  • A pull that returns zero rows never overwrites good data.
  • Run reports and attempt logs are committed, so the history is auditable.
