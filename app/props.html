Two −110 legs pay 3.645 decimal, so each leg must win 52.38% to break even even when the legs are independent. Negative correlation makes it worse, not better: correlated legs that move together lose together.

The arithmetic, stated plainly

  • A single −110 bet breaks even at 52.38%.
  • A two-leg −110 parlay pays about 3.645 decimal and breaks even at 72.11% joint — which needs each leg at 52.38% only if the legs are independent.
  • Correlation moves the joint probability toward the Fréchet bounds. For QB passing yards and WR/TE receiving yards (r = 0.927) the joint hit rate is 51.2%, still above the 47.6% the price implies — but for RB rushing yards paired with a receiver prop (r = −0.163) the joint is 24.0%, well below it, and the combination is unprofitable at any stake.
  • The lesson the gates encode: a parlay is not a way to manufacture edge. It multiplies whatever edge or disadvantage each leg already has, plus the book's margin on every leg.
