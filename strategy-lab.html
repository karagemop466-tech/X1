Validation gates

Every strategy must pass every gate that applies to it. A gate whose threshold is marked not set is deliberately unenforced rather than given a plausible-looking number: an invented threshold is worse than no threshold because it looks like evidence.

Removing luck from the equation

The brief asks for strategies that are selectively skill based. Concretely, that means four things, in this order:

  1. Pre-registration. The hypothesis, inputs and staking rule are recorded before anything is graded. A strategy chosen after seeing results is not evidence of skill, it is evidence of memory. Enforced by gate G-02.
  2. Sample size before promotion. A short run of wins at standard NFL prices happens constantly by chance. Nothing goes live before the minimum graded-bet count is reached. Gate G-03, threshold pending Q-07.
  3. Beat the price, not just the game. Winning bets is not the same as having an edge; the test is whether the hit rate beats the break-even rate implied by the price taken. Gate G-12.
  4. Correct for how many strategies were tried. With enough candidates, one will look skilled by chance alone. The number of live strategies is capped and significance is corrected for it. Gate G-14.
Closing line value (gate G-11) is the single most useful measure here, because it scores a signal without waiting for the result: if our originated line consistently beats the number the market closes at, the signal contains information the market did not. It needs a price feed, which no official source provides (Q-02).

Rewards

The brief asks for rewards for top performing strategies. No reward rule exists yet, and inventing one would change the incentive structure of the leaderboard without the owner's input. The rule is defined by Q-06, and the mechanics are proposed as S-06: score signal quality and realised return on separate leaderboards, so a strategy cannot win the reward by taking a lucky large position.
