Why these numbers matter

1. Parlays: the variance ratio (0.99) empirically justifies treating different games as independent legs. If slate-level shocks (weather systems, refs, weeks) mattered, this ratio would exceed 1.0 — it does not. 2. Same-game parlays: the spread↔total Δ (+0.3pp) shows even the two markets inside one game are weakly coupled at the *game* level. Player-level coupling (QB↔WR etc.) is larger but unmeasured — blocked until play-by-play data exists (props-parlays.html). 3. Own lines: corr(spread, margin) = 0.431 quantifies how much information the market line already contains; any model claiming an edge must demonstrate |model − market| disagreements that survive the gates.

Caveats

  • All values are market-era regular-season aggregates; sub-period shifts are checked in Gate F, not here.
  • Player-prop correlations: no values published — data blocked (OQ-4). Any number you see elsewhere until then is not from this system.
