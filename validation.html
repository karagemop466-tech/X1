Competition design

  • Leaderboard entries are versioned strategies with a fixed submission deadline and fully timestamped forecasts. Include every qualifying wager and pass; prohibit retroactive edits.
  • Separate development, public practice, and private/hidden evaluation data. Cap submissions and use a final untouched season or rolling hidden windows to reduce leaderboard overfitting.
  • Show net return alongside sample size, confidence interval, calibration, closing-line comparison where valid, max drawdown, exposure concentration, and record completeness. Never sort by raw win rate alone.
  • Predefine minimum volume and risk-adjusted reward formula before launch; pay no reward on insufficient evidence. Require reproducibility and independent audit before payout.
  • For parlays, evaluate joint probability, combined price and risk; include them in a separate cohort so high variance does not dominate single-bet rankings.

Remove luck? The honest answer

Luck cannot be removed from finite outcomes. It can be measured and controlled for: favor probability forecasts over short-run wins, use chronological out-of-sample tests, compare to strong baselines, quantify uncertainty, account for searching many strategies, and require replication. No finite leaderboard proves skill with certainty. Reward claims must state residual uncertainty.

Not yet defined: sample-size thresholds, confidence level, net-return metric, allowed markets, submission limits, reward pool, and eligibility/compliance rules need decisions and domain/legal review. No arbitrary values are filled in here.
