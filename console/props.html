Props and parlays

The mathematics of combining legs, and why no prop strategy is scored.

No player-prop lines exist in any source available here. nflverse/nfldata does not carry them and the league publishes no betting lines of any kind. The requirement asks for player-prop combinations. Publishing a prop model without prop prices would mean inventing the prices, so this page publishes the mathematics and an empty results table instead. See X-02 and SG-07.

What can be said without prices

  • A parlay is the product of its legs. Two -110 legs return 3.6446 for 1, so the joint probability has to exceed 27.44%.
  • Legs that are positively correlated are worth more than the product implies, and legs that are independent are worth exactly the product. The measured spread/total correlation is 0.0197 (n=6,115), which is close enough to zero that a same-game side/total parlay is not mispriced.
  • The vig compounds. At -110 each leg carries a 4.55% margin; parlaying two legs applies that margin to both, and books then discount the payout further. The measured result on this board is a -36.6% flat return on 891 bets.
  • Correlated same-game legs are the one case where a parlay can be priced wrong, and measuring that needs the book's actual correlated-parlay price. No source here publishes one.
