What this page is. The brief asks for a two-sided "what would change our mind" field on every strategy entry. The lab leaderboard carries one side — a falsifier, the observation that would make us abandon an entry — and the console strategy table carries neither. One-sided is how a losing strategy survives a review cycle: no bar is stated for closing it, and no bar is stated for adopting it, so the test just continues. This page states both, as arithmetic, under a rule frozen in Git before the week it might be read against.

The three findings this page makes possible

  • 7 entries are already past their abandon bound. Their own published interval sits entirely under break-even: ST-HOMEDOG, ST-SIDE-3, ST-BOTH-3, ST-SIDE-5, ST-PARLAY-4, S-02-total-2.0, S-02-total-1.0. Two of them had a falsifier on file naming this exact condition — it has fired, and the entry is still open on the board. That is what a one-sided field costs.
  • 19 entries cannot be promoted by more data. Their observed rate sits below the bar, so the honest statement is "no n exists at this rate", not a sample size. Printing a number for those would invite exactly the move this page exists to prevent: run the test longer until the interval happens to clear.
  • Nothing is close. The cheapest undecided adoption is 928 more bets (S-04-parlay-2) and the cheapest undecided abandonment is 1,487 (ST-REST2), against a corpus that produced a few thousand bets per entry over a whole season. The economics of the test, not the model, is what makes most of these dead. 2 rows are suspended outright because the table’s published interval and this register’s recomputation disagree (ST-ML-10, ST-ML-5).

Definitions, in the rule’s words

TermAs the register defines it
adoptthe entry's published 95% interval on (rate - bar) lies entirely above zero
abandonthat interval lies entirely below zero
basisthe interval is the one the entry's own table publishes; strategy_results.csv publishes CI_LOW/CI_HIGH on the edge scale, the lab leaderboard publishes hit_rate_ci95 on the rate scale, converted here against the same bar
bets to adoptn* = ceil((z/|p-p0|)^2 * p(1-p)) at the observed rate - the sample that would clear the bar without the rate moving; a lower bound where the published interval is wider than the normal approximation
rate to adoptp* = p0 + z*sqrt(p0(1-p0)/n) - the rate that clears the bar at today's n
no nwhen the observed rate is on the wrong side of the bar, more data cannot promote the entry, and the register says that instead of printing a large n

Why the bar cannot be moved after the fact

The rule text is docs/preregistration/MIND-CHANGE-01.md, registered 2026-11-18 — before the first Week 11 kickoff, which the verifier asserts by comparing that date with the escrow file’s earliest kickoff. Every row carries the SHA-256 of the file as it stood at build time (1b9cfc56261e7609), and python3 tools/verify_mind_change.py fails if the rows and the file on disk disagree. Re-running the builder on a later date changes every number here from the upstream tables, which is the point: this page is a view, not a record.
