Executive summary

What was built, what the evidence says, and what has to change before anything goes live.

Executive summary

The brief asks for eight things: a price-snapshot store, a verified results feed, origination in the order side → total → moneyline, three published columns (fair, market, difference), signal and staking scored apart, replacement depth rather than replacement, correlations computed from sourced numbers with n published, and a demotion gate. All eight are built and published. The competition they run produces a negative result, and the negative result is the finding.

1. The model does not beat the market, and the market can prove it

The closing spread correlates 0.434 with the actual home margin (n=6,102). Our walk-forward fair spread correlates 0.2332. In points, our fair line misses the actual margin by 14.198 RMSE against the market's 13.14 — about 1.1 points per game worse than the number we are trying to beat.

The decisive test is whether the market moves toward our number. It does not. In 2021, the one season where a real opening number exists, the gap between our fair line and the market grew from 4.037 points at the open to 5.545 points at the close — a change of +1.508 points with a standard error of 0.229 over n=264 games. The gap shrank in only 30% of them. If our number carried information the opening line lacked, the close would have moved toward it. It moved away, by about six standard errors. The disagreement is our error, not the market's.

2. Every strategy fails the gates, which is what the gates are for

16 strategies were pre-registered and evaluated out of sample from 2004 to 2026, with every hyper-parameter re-chosen inside the training window only. None passed. The best flat-stake return on the board is -0.60% on 2,781 bets, against break-even at 52.38% and three always-on baselines that lose between 5.2% and 6.9%. A parlay strategy returned -36.5%.

Two results deserve to be read closely. First, bigger disagreement does not mean better bets: the correlation between edge size and bet outcome is zero or negative for every strategy, and significantly negative for the largest side threshold (-0.069 , 95% CI -0.125 to -0.011). Second, parlays multiply the vig: with the two legs effectively uncorrelated (0.0199, n=6,102), a two-leg -110 parlay needs a 27.44% joint probability to break even and returns less at every book.

3. The data underneath is sound

7,413 settled games reconcile 160/160 with the league's own standings table. The official pages were re-read today: 859 comparisons, 8 mismatches — all of them on the one register whose table is a dated snapshot rather than a re-read (see standings). The market's own moneylines imply its own spreads to within 0.516 points (n=5,432). The benchmark we are failing is a real one.

4. Signal and staking are now separable, and it matters

The leaderboard reports signal_score at flat one-unit staking, which bet sizing cannot move, and staking_score under the strategy's own quarter-Kelly plan. They disagree. ST-TOT-5 and ST-ROADFAV have the identical signal score of 25.0, yet they sit 10.2 points apart on staking (35.2 against 25.0). A leaderboard ranked on staking would order them; the signal column says they are the same bet. Promotion requires the signal score, so neither is promoted.

5. Availability tracking works for 31 of 32 clubs

897 depth slots. 189 designated starters appear on the official Week 10 injury report, 39 of them Out, 4 doubtful and 35 questionable. Each row names the replacement and whether he is the 2nd, 3rd or 4th string, with a link to the club page it was read from. Houston publishes no parsable chart and is recorded as a coverage gap rather than filled in.

6. What has to change for any of this to pay

Three gaps block every remaining question: no source here publishes opening prices, none publishes player-prop lines, and none publishes Houston's depth chart. The first two are commercial; the third is a one-off transcription. Until opening prices exist, closing line value — the single best stake-free measure of whether a signal knows something — cannot be computed, and the demotion gate cannot be exercised on real money. The suggestion board prices each path.

The recommendation

Do not promote anything, and do not raise the bet threshold to make the numbers look better. Spend the next cycle on the price feed (SG-01) and player participation (SG-02). Both are cheap, and both are prerequisites for every other question on the board. The gates are working; the inputs are what is missing.
