A strategy that mostly backs underdogs has a low hit rate and a low bar; one that mostly backs favourites has the opposite. Raw hit rates are not comparable across the two, which is exactly why the bar is per ticket.

Why signal and staking are different columns

Signal: the picks only

  • n and hit rate on decided tickets; pushes excluded from the denominator.
  • The break-even bar is per ticket. A -110 single breaks even at 52.38%; a +250 moneyline at 28.6%; a two-leg -110 parlay at 27.44%. Testing everything against 52.38% scored the parlay at z = -7.2 when it was actually 3 points above its own bar.
  • z is a Poisson-binomial test, because each ticket has its own bar.
  • Interval: 2,000 bootstrap resamples, fixed seed.
  • Luck trim: one-score games and blowouts removed, because a single bounce decides those.

Staking: the money only

  • ROI at a flat unit, and again under a 5%-of-bankroll compounding plan.
  • Max drawdown under the compounding plan, against a 25% pre-registered cap.
  • Independence: a profit that exists under one stake plan and not the other is labelled as an artefact rather than a result.
