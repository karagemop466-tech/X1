SNAPSHOT 2026-11-14 · WEEK 10
The order is not cosmetic. Side first, then the total, then the moneyline. A moneyline is a probability built from the same scoring distribution that produces the other two, so deriving it last makes it a consequence of two numbers that are already checked against the market. Deriving it first would mean inventing a probability and reverse-engineering a spread from it.

The honest verdict, in the model's own words

The ratings model carries real information: it beats the naive 'home team by 2.5' baseline on side MAE. It does not beat the market. That is the expected result for a model with no information the market does not have, and it is published rather than hidden. No stake is staked on this model; it is a screen and the benchmark for the strategy layer.

Three defects this model had, and how they were caught

IDWhat was wrongEffectFixCode
L-01sample weight applied to the target only, not to the design matrixtotal MAE published as 28.413 on a quantity averaging 44; the fit was shrinking toward zero rather than weighting recent gamesridge() now accumulates w in both X'X and X'ybuild_lines.py
L-02the current-week board selected max(season, week) among scheduled games and published week 18the 'this week' board was not this weekthe board now takes the earliest week with unplayed games in the latest season with any unplayed gamesbuild_lines.py
