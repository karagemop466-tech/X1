The three paths, with their cost

Path idThe pathWhat it costsWhat it risksArtefact
Await for the mirror0 games stay unsettled in the feed for an unknown period; no gate changesthe official score is already printed and cross-checked; waiting buys nothing but delaymeasurement
Blet an official read settle a row when the mirror has no row, and flag ita change in tools/build_results_r3.py: the mirror becomes a cross-check where present instead of a veto; the M3 standings closure stays mandatorythe row is then verified by one official page plus arithmetic rather than by two sources; the artefact must say so on every rowmeasurement
Cpromote the arithmetic closure to the primary gatethe 32/32 standings closure becomes required for every row and the mirror becomes advisory everywhere, not only where it is missinga whole-round transcription error could still close arithmetically if it is self-consistent; the row-level second source is what B and C give upmeasurement

B is the recommended path and it is small: one gate edit, a re-pinned baseline, the row-count pins, and a regeneration of the artefacts in M5. What it is not is a data problem - M3 shows the closure reproduces the league table exactly. No session should make that call, because it changes what 'verified' means for every row already published; that is why it is recorded here as a decision and not implemented as a change.

What is already true, and is not usually said out loud. The closure arithmetic passes: the 150 games this repository can show reproduce all 32 official standings rows exactly — wins, losses, ties, points for and points against, for every club, against the league table read on 2026-11-17. So this is not a question of whether the Week 10 scores are right. It is a question of what the word verified is allowed to mean for a row: two independent sources, or one official page plus the arithmetic that only closes if every row is present. That is the owner's call, and it is the one this round recorded instead of taking.
